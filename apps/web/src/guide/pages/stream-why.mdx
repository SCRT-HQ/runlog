# A stream run by dice

A stream needs a next thing to happen. Runlog hands you one: roll on a table, take the twist, do it on camera, and the app remembers. It is neither a virtual tabletop nor a wheel; it is a game of rounds and consequences beside whatever you already stream, with the state on screen and the log kept.

## What it looks like on a stream

- **Every death, a twist.** A challenge pack for the game you play draws a handicap each region, a curse that lands on the whole roster, a target worth points to whoever settles it first. **Elden Ring: TarnishedTool** and **Rocket League: Mechanics Ladder** are built this way for one player; **Rocket League: Showdown** for a room; **Forfeits** is a penalty wheel for whatever you are playing, spun on every third death.
- **Chat as the roster.** In a [moderated run](#guide/moderated) you sit at the app and everyone else is a name on a scoreboard, no accounts for them. Award the points as they land; the standings sit on the scene.
- **A race between channels.** Two streamers on the same seeded mode get the same dice without meeting; the [race](#guide/races) leaderboard shows who is ahead.
- **A twist on something real.** The packs that ship cover a kitchen, a studio, a practice room and a day, and the Designer writes yours without touching a file.
- **Consequences that reach back.** A result can land on something made an hour ago; a state can pile up; "after you finish, roll a d6" is not forgotten.

## What it needs

A browser, a streaming app with a browser source (all of them have one, and [TikTok LIVE Studio](#guide/tiktok) calls it a Link), and a pack. Chat can press too: a channel-point reward on Twitch or [Kick](#guide/kick), a gift on TikTok, a command anywhere, asks the run for a spin or a move. Compared with the rest:

|                              | Runlog                                                         | A virtual tabletop                                                                                                                     | A wheel or dice overlay           |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| To a first run               | Open the app; one minute, no account                           | An account for you and each player, a system, a map, tokens; between a quarter of an hour and an evening, more if you host it yourself | Instant                           |
| To a widget on a scene       | A live link and one address in a browser source; a few minutes | Screen capture, or a community module                                                                                                  | Instant                           |
| Remembers what came up       | Every roll, every result, whether it was done, exportable      | The campaign, if you write it down                                                                                                     | No; a few keep a list of results  |
| Consequences on earlier work | Yes; results that reach back, states, counters, deferred rolls | By hand                                                                                                                                | No                                |
| A scoreboard or a race       | Built in; a roster from chat, or channels racing on one seed   | No                                                                                                                                     | No                                |
| The twists come from         | A pack: yours, one that ships, or one from the marketplace     | Maps and modules bought from its store                                                                                                 | Whatever you typed into the wheel |
| Viewers need                 | Nothing; a link watches                                        | Nothing to watch a capture                                                                                                             | Nothing                           |

The tabletop column is the platform a group of role-players meets in, with a map and character sheets; Runlog does not try to be one. If your game is a battle map, use one of those. If your game is "the dice decide what I do next, and it counts," that is this.

## Alongside a chaos platform

Crowd Control and the rest are a remote control for viewers: chat pays, and something happens to the game. Runlog answers a different question, which is what the run says happens next and whether it counts. Neither replaces the other, and a stream can carry both.

Runlog is the referee, and anything can be the hand that acts: you, a tool beside the game, or chat itself. What the run counts is the same either way.

## The first stream, in order

<Steps>
  <li>Open the app and play a round of any challenge pack alone, to see the shape of it; no account needed.</li>
  <li>
    Sign in, open the run, and **Share a live link** <Plan tier="plus" />. Anyone with the link can watch; that is also what a widget reads.
  </li>
  <li>
    In **Settings** → **Stream**, tick **For another machine**, set the background to *Clear*, and **Copy address** on the Ticker, the
    Scoreboard or the Clock.
  </li>
  <li>
    Paste it into a browser source. [OBS Studio and Streamlabs](#guide/obs) has the fields; [StreamElements](#guide/streamelements) and
    [Streamer.bot](#guide/streamer-bot) each have a page.
  </li>
  <li>Go live. The widget follows the run within a second while the socket is up.</li>
</Steps>

Undo is a move like any other, so a misclick on stream is a press, not a restart. The dock keeps the run's remote beside your preview in OBS: [a dock for the controls](#guide/dock).

## Where the twists come from

The packs that ship are free and in the marketplace. Write your own in [the Designer](#guide/design): the vocabulary, the tables, what each result does, the points a target is worth. A pack is a small file that prints as a rulebook and can be [sold in the marketplace](#guide/selling), sealed to the buyer, with the ledger kept for you.
