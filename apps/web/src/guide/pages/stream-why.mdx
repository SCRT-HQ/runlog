# A stream run by dice

A stream needs a next thing to happen. Runlog hands you one: roll on a table, take the twist, do it on camera, and the app remembers. It is not a virtual tabletop and not a wheel. It is the piece between them that neither provides: a game of rounds and consequences that runs beside whatever you already stream, with the state on screen and the log kept.

## What it looks like on a stream

- **Every death, a twist.** A challenge pack for the game you play draws a handicap each region, a curse that lands on the whole roster, a target worth points to whoever settles it first. **Elden Ring: Expedition** and **Rocket League: Mechanics Ladder** are built this way for one player; **Elden Ring: Trial** and **Rocket League: Showdown** for a room.
- **Chat as the roster.** In a [moderated run](#guide/moderated) you sit at the app and everyone else is a name on a scoreboard, no accounts for them. Award the points as they land; the standings sit on the scene.
- **A race between channels.** Two streamers on the same seeded mode get the same dice without ever meeting; the [race](#guide/races) leaderboard shows who is ahead.
- **A twist on something real.** Cooking under constraint, a drawing an hour, an hour at the wheel, a house cleaned like a dungeon. The packs that ship cover a kitchen, a studio, a practice room and a day, and the Designer writes yours without touching a file.
- **Consequences that reach back.** A result can land on something made an hour ago; a state can pile up; "after you finish, roll a d6" is not forgotten. That is what a wheel cannot do and what makes a run a story rather than a list of spins.

## What it needs

A browser, a streaming app with a browser source (all of them have one), and a pack. Nothing to install, no plugin, nothing for a viewer to sign up for. Compared with the rest:

| | Runlog | A virtual tabletop | A wheel or dice overlay | A chaos platform |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| To a first run | Open the app; one minute, no account | An account for you and each player, a system, a map, tokens; between a quarter of an hour and an evening, more if you host it yourself | Instant | An app, an account, a game the platform supports |
| To a widget on a scene | A live link and one address in a browser source; a few minutes | Screen capture, or a community module | Instant | Its own overlay |
| Remembers what came up | Every roll, every result, whether it was done, exportable | The campaign, if you write it down | No; a few keep a list of results | No run log |
| Consequences on earlier work | Yes; results that reach back, states, counters, deferred rolls | By hand | No | No |
| A scoreboard or a race | Built in; a roster from chat, or channels racing on one seed | No | No | Leaderboards of who paid |
| The twists come from | A pack: yours, one that ships, or one from the catalog | Maps and modules bought from its store | Whatever you typed into the wheel | The vendor's effect list |
| Viewers need | Nothing; a link watches | Nothing to watch a capture | Nothing | Coins, bits or points to take part |

The tabletop column is the kind of platform a group of role-players meets in, with a map and character sheets. It is very good at that, and Runlog does not try to be. If your game is a battle map, use one of those. If your game is "the dice decide what I do next, and it counts," that is this.

## The first stream, in order

<Steps>
<li>Open the app and play a round of any challenge pack, alone, to see the shape of it. No account for this.</li>
<li>Sign in, open the run, and **Share a live link** <Plan tier="plus" />. Anyone with the link can watch; that is also what a widget reads.</li>
<li>In **Settings** → **Stream**, tick **For another machine**, set the background to *Clear*, and **Copy address** on the Ticker, the Scoreboard or the Clock.</li>
<li>Paste it into a browser source. [OBS Studio and Streamlabs](#guide/obs) has the fields; [StreamElements](#guide/streamelements) and [Streamer.bot](#guide/streamer-bot) each have a page.</li>
<li>Go live. The widget follows the run within a second while the socket is up.</li>
</Steps>

Undo is a move like any other, so a misclick on stream is a press, not a restart. The dock keeps the run's remote beside your preview in OBS: [a dock for the controls](#guide/dock).

## Where the twists come from

The packs that ship are free and in the catalog. Write your own in [the Designer](#guide/design): the vocabulary, the tables, what each result does, the points a target is worth. A pack is a small file, so a challenge set for your channel travels with you, prints as a rulebook, and can be [sold in the catalog](#guide/selling) to other streamers, sealed to the buyer, with the ledger kept for you.

<Note>Where plans are switched on, the live link and the widgets are part of Plus, like hosting a table: a stream is a table with an audience. Playing, the Designer, and every pack that ships are free.</Note>
