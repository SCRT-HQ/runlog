# A seat at another table

Someone invited to **play** a run of a pack they do not own gets a seat. The run opens on the watching page, with a strip of what you can press. Every press travels to the device that has the run open, which makes the move and sends the result back.

## What you see

Results, the board, the log and the scoreboard, as they happen. Not the pack: its tables, its steps and its text stay with whoever owns it. The header says which pack is being played and links to its card in the marketplace where it has one.

## What you can press

- On a pack played by a table, the run's next press and its moves, when it is your turn.
- On a solo or moderated pack, the one seat is the host's. You answer what a step asks, move the tallies and the dials, and send a reaction.
- Taking a move back, handing out a setup, the clock, the dice setting and ending the run are the host's, on every pack.

## When nobody has the run open

The strip says **Waiting for the run to be opened.** and goes quiet. A seat plays through the host's page, so the host has to have it on screen.

## Your own copy

Once the pack is in your library, bought or added the way any pack is, opening the run puts it on your own copy instead of a seat: the whole pack, its paper, and moves that travel between you and the rest of the table the way they do on any run played together.

<Note>
  An author can say their pack is a copy each. On one of those, an invitation to play goes out as an invitation to watch, and the invitation
  says so.
</Note>
