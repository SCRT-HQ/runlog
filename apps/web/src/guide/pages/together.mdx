# Playing together

Runlog plays alone by default. Signing in adds three things: your runs on every device, other people in your runs, and a run one person moderates for a room.

## On every device <Plan tier="free" />

Sign in and turn on **Sync on this device** in the menu. Your runs go to your account and come back to your other devices; the one you touched last is what **Continue where you left off** opens. Sync is on by default once you sign in, and can be turned off per device.

## Inviting someone <Plan tier="plus" />

In any run, **Invite someone** in the side column sends a link by email. Whoever opens it, signed in as that address, joins the run as a **player** or a **watcher**:

- A **player** makes moves. Two devices with the same run open see each other's moves within a second — the hosted copy keeps a line open while a run is on screen — and both can undo their own.
- A **watcher** sees every move and makes none. Good for a stream, a coach, a friend following along.

People you have played with are remembered, so the next invitation is a name rather than an address. The owner of a run can remove anyone from it.

## Inviting someone to Runlog <Plan tier="free" />

**Invite a friend** on your profile sends one mail with a link to sign up, and nothing else, ever, to that address. It is the same door as Create an account, with your name on the invitation.

## Moderated play <Plan tier="plus" />

Some games are played by people who cannot hold the device — they are in the match, or on stream. A **moderated** mode puts one person at the app and everyone else on a roster of names, no accounts needed:

- Every result the round draws lands on everyone: a curse, a handicap, a twist.
- The results that carry **points** are the challenges. **Winners** is a grid: a row per challenge, a column per contestant, a cell to press when someone finishes. Under "first to finish", a row holds one winner and pressing another cell moves the award; under "everyone finishes", each cell is its own award, the first marked ★ for the bonus.
- The **scoreboard** keeps standings, ties sharing a place. The moderator can add a late arrival, drop someone, or mark a contestant — spared from a curse, out of this region — from the row.
- Watchers see the grid and the scoreboard and press nothing.

Among the packs built this way: **Rocket League: Showdown** and **Elden Ring: Trial**.

## A live link <Plan tier="plus" />

Under **People at the table**, **Share a live link** gives you an address anyone can open, with no account, to watch the run as it happens: the log, the board, the clocks, the scoreboard. **Stop sharing** kills the link. What a viewer sees of the pack follows its license: a pack whose text may travel is shown whole, its paper a press away; a pack marked not for redistribution shows the state and the log by reference, never the rules. A watcher is not a member: they cannot move, and they are not in the run's people.

## Races across devices <Plan tier="plus" />

A **race** is several people playing the same seeded mode at once, each in a run of their own on their own device. The seed hands everyone the same dice, so the runs agree without ever meeting; what travels is each racer's progress. On a seeded mode's setup screen, **Start a race** gives you a six-letter code; anyone with the pack joins by typing it under **Race** on their setup screen, or by opening a link with it. The side column shows the leaderboard — who has finished, who is furthest along, who is fastest — and the one who started the race can mail the code or end the race. **Elden Ring: Expedition**'s seeded race is built for this.

Streaming it? The widgets under **Stream** in the side column put the scoreboard, the clock, or the race on pages of their own; see [Streaming a run](#guide/streaming).

<Note>Where plans are switched on, the badges say what needs Plus: hosting a table. Where they are not — the preview, a copy you run yourself — everything here is open to everyone. Your profile says which it is, under Plan.</Note>
